Cloud-native applications rely on third-party components whose runtime behavior can change after deployment. Monitors and software bills of materials (SBOMs) capture different evidence; linking events to components and causal responsibility remains difficult. PHANTOM implements SBOM verification interfaces, eBPF event collection, variable-order Markov behavioral contracts, a time-decayed Behavioral Dependency Graph (BDG), and a DoWhy-based causal-estimation pathway and an XGBoost PCEPS scorer. Its design combines image- and PURL-scoped contract generation with a temporal causal projection that returns not_identifiable when specified identification checks fail. This is an integration design, not a claim that these mechanisms are new or that component-level causal attribution has been validated. In an evaluation of six completed attack runs across the two tested supply-chain attack families, PHANTOM recorded drift events with mean latency 15.74 seconds, median 5.52 seconds, and linearly interpolated 95th percentile 54.72 seconds. Three SolarWinds-style injections failed before an attack phase. The saved event records lack resolved PURL/SBOM bindings, and the dataset contains no benign controls; thus attribution accuracy, false-positive rate, calibrated prediction, and runtime overhead remain unestablished. These preliminary results document event presence and latency only. PHANTOM contributes an implementable, uncertainty-aware research pathway whose component mapping and causal value require controlled evaluation.
