Cloud-native services combine large numbers of third-party components, and a malicious replacement can preserve a package's public identity while changing what it does after deployment. The resulting runtime deviation may be visible to kernel monitors even when conventional alerts do not identify its software origin. Existing tools rarely link inventories, behavior, and causal explanations. This design keeps component identity, event-level evidence, and causal uncertainty visible to operators during investigation and response. PHANTOM associates SBOM components with privacy-preserving behavioral contracts learned from eBPF event sequences. It constructs a time-aware Behavioral Dependency Graph from process, file, and network events, then projects that graph into a structural causal model for component-level analysis. A separate pre-compromise scorer ranks workloads for investigation, while returning an explicit not-identifiable outcome when the available graph cannot support a causal estimate. PHANTOM achieves a true-positive rate of 0.889, a false-positive rate of 0.050, and an F1 score of 0.872. Its mean time to detection is 54.3 seconds, with 3.1\% mean CPU overhead and P95 event lag of 23 ms, remaining within practical runtime budgets for production Kubernetes deployments. PHANTOM contributes an integrated design for runtime SBOM drift detection and cautious causal attribution. It provides traceable evidence for analyst review.\n
